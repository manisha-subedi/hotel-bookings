Two hotels, 119,390 bookings

A resort hotel in the Algarve and a city hotel in Lisbon. Three years of real bookings, July 2015 to August 2017. This page has three parts: the numbers a manager looks at every month, a lookup for how often a booking like this one cancels, and a tool that says how many rooms to oversell.

3. How many rooms to oversell

A hotel that sells exactly its room count ends most nights with empty rooms, because some bookings cancel. Selling a few more is normal. The question is how many. Too few, and rooms sit empty. Too many, and a guest with a booking has to be sent to another hotel, which costs money and goodwill.

The rate that matters is not the overall 37 percent. Most of those cancellations happen weeks before arrival, when the room can still be sold again. What matters is how many bookings still on the books a few days before arrival will cancel or not show up. For the city hotel that is 8.5 percent a week out, for the resort 4.8 percent. Pick the horizon, or type your own rate.

Then set the cost of an empty room and the cost of sending a guest away. The chart shows the expected cost per night for each number of extra bookings. The lowest point is the answer.

How it is built

The bookings file is one flat table with 32 columns. The first step reshapes it into a star schema in DuckDB: one fact table of bookings, one of occupied room nights, and four dimension tables. Arrival date and cancellation date both point at the same date table.

The room count is not in the data, so it is estimated as the most rooms ever occupied on one night: 226 for the city hotel, 187 for the resort. Occupancy is rooms sold over rooms available. A common mistake is to divide by booked nights instead, which gives 100 percent every month. A test in the repo fails if that ever happens.

Everything on this page is computed from the tables at build time and written as small JSON files. The page reads them and draws the charts. No model, no server. The code, the SQL, and the tests are on GitHub.
